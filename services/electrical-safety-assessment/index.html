A useful assessment has a stated purpose and boundary

A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. A general visit should not be represented as complete certification of everything in the property.

Prepare the scope for your Fort Erie property

Collect any home inspection report, such as one from a recent purchase, and highlight every electrical item it raised. Write down the one system or area you want reviewed, and list any earlier work you know about but cannot document.

Ask the contractor which equipment and areas are inside the review, whether any testing is included and what is left out. Ask what form the written result will take and whether it can be shared with an insurer or lender.

What should the assessment establish?

What exactly was examined in the one system or area reviewed in Fort Erie, and what was never reached? The report lists the parts examined and those not reached, and describes nothing beyond them. Which findings come from visible equipment, and which rest on concealed wiring that could not be seen? Each finding is marked with its source, and any limit is written beside it. What records, labels and ESA notifications turned up for that part? Those are listed, with items needing attention kept apart from simple observations.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is a review of one area the same as a whole-home inspection?

No. It covers only the equipment and area named in the scope, and its conclusions stop there. If you need the whole property considered, say so in your enquiry so the scope can be set accordingly.

What should I do with items raised in a purchase inspection?

Have the report ready with each electrical item marked. Ask the contractor to say which items fall inside the assessment and which need separate work, and keep the answer with your records.
